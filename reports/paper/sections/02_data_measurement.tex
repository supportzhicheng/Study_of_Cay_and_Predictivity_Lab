\section{Data and Construction of \cay}

The empirical construction uses quarterly consumption, labor income, and
household net worth. Each series is converted to a real per-capita natural
log before estimating the common trend.

\subsection{Data Sources}

Consumption and the components used to construct labor income come from the
U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis National Income and Product Accounts,
accessed directly or through FRED \citep{bea_nipa,fred}. Household net worth
comes from the Federal Reserve Financial Accounts, or Z.1
\citep{federal_reserve_z1}. S\&P Composite prices, dividends, and earnings are
obtained from Robert Shiller's data; CRSP value-weighted returns and the
Treasury-bill series are obtained through WRDS/CRSP
\citep{shiller_data,crsp_data}. The author-posted \cay{} series is used only as
a validation target.
The source manifest and generated metadata record exact series identifiers,
table descriptions, transformations, download dates, and vintages. Licensed
and copyrighted source files remain outside Git.

\subsection{Dynamic Least Squares}

The cointegrating vector is estimated using dynamic least squares with leads
and lags of changes in asset wealth and labor income \citep{stock_watson_1993}.
The paper reports
\[
c_t = 0.61 + 0.31a_t + 0.59y_t,
\]
and defines the reported predictor without subtracting the intercept:
\[
cay_t = c_t - 0.31a_t - 0.59y_t.
\]

\subsection{Validation}

The historical construction uses \CayHistoricalDefinition{} over
\HistoricalSampleStart--\HistoricalSampleEnd. Coefficient, posted-series, and
exhibit discrepancies are evaluated by the generated replication audit rather
than by visual similarity. The current aggregate audit status is
\texttt{\OverallReplicationStatus}.
